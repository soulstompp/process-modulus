What does the model have to be bent to say?

> **Também disponível em português europeu:** `pt-PT/examples/strain/README.md`.

The other programs ask whether the queries are right. This one assumes they are and goes looking
for ordinary situations, from ordinary businesses, that the schema has no honest way to say. A
field that has to be bent to take a fact is a finding about the field, and worth more than another
document that fits.

It changes one thing the model takes for granted at a time, keeps everything else, and prints what
a filing would then have to claim. It accuses no filing and needs no database.

## Three timescales, and the model files two

Three timescales bear on any figure here:

- **the whole unit** supply arrives in, filed with the origin of who sets it;
- **the period** a figure is quoted over: per quarter, per week;
- **the timescale on which the figure actually moves.** No element carries it.

The third matters because a range here reads as what nobody knows, and `narrowsWhen` says what
would tighten it. A figure that genuinely varies week to week does not narrow when you measure
harder, and the two readings are not told apart. That is the open question. A period on every
range would touch every place that reads a range's width, and a flag choosing between not knowing
and varying is a false choice, since the honest answer is usually both. If you see another way, it
is the most useful thing you could send back.

## A window folds into a rate, and its duration does not survive

A line live from 02:00 to 05:00, making one unit every five seconds, has a nameplate of 2,160 a
day. Against a demand of 2,000 a day it has 160 a day to spare, whatever the schedule. How long a
wait that spare means does not survive into the rate: 68 seconds inside the window, 21 hours
outside it, and no duration worked out from the daily rate occurs anywhere in the schedule. So a
slack is filed as a quantity in the layer's unit, never as the duration it was observed as, and
the filer does the converting.

## A window is carried, never added up

Two members naming one machine file one calendar between them. Adding their windows would say the
machine runs ten days a week. A window belongs to the supply, not to a quantity, which is why an
elimination removes demand, nameplate or draw, and never a window. `checks/window_lost_or_summed`
is the rule.

## Running it

```text
cargo run --example strain
```
